## 02 · THE PROBLEMS — do they understand what I'm experiencing?

Moves to the front, per both. Michael: bare titles aren't enough — explain each. So:
nine problems, but each carries its consequence in one sentence (no floating pills —
design note for later, Michael dislikes the slow-moving objects).

Keep Michael's nine, one line of "we get it" copy each:
  1. Shipping costs keep rising — carrier increases and surcharges land faster than you can reprice.
  2. Customers expect Amazon-like delivery — two-day and live tracking became the baseline, whether or not your operation was built for it.
  3. Hard to find a competent 3PL — DIY doesn't scale, but outsourcing feels like losing control of your customer promise.
  4. Logistics gets more complicated with growth — every channel, SKU and region adds moving parts, and service suffers right when volume spikes.
  5. Where should inventory go? — placement decisions get made by habit and warehouse capacity, not demand.
  6. Which carriers bring the best value? — the answer changes by lane, weight and season; nobody has time to re-shop it.
  7. Optimizing for the wrong stage — what worked at $5M actively hurts at $30M.
  8. Returns become less manageable — reverse logistics grows faster than forward and lands on whoever has least time.
  9. Customer experience degrades — the operation's stress becomes the customer's problem, and they don't come back.

Closer (sets up the pivot): It isn't a discipline problem. Growing companies struggle
with logistics because it's a *system design* problem — and nobody sold you a system.

## 05 · HOW WE WORK — the sell process as the narrative spine (NEW, replaces "solution" ordering)

Chris's five steps, verbatim intent, presented as the way we engage. Human element
lives here (Michael: people shots at review/design, onboarding, support; Chris:
"experienced operators, not account managers"):

  1. **Understand** — your business, your customers, the facts on the ground.
  2. **Assess** — your logistics maturity; model the scenarios. (→ Logistics Performance Score lives here)
  3. **Design** — the right operating model for your goals, priorities and stage. Sometimes the whole system, sometimes one component first.
  4. **Implement** — with our technology, network partners and carrier ecosystem.
  5. **Optimize** — continuously, through data and hands-on execution. This never stops.

Design target (per Michael's brief): the operating system delivers on the
**Performance Triangle** — cost, delivery times, customer experience. (Triangle
graphic stays, now named.)
